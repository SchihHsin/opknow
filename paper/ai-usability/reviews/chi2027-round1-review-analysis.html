Reviewer 4 (AC) · The Meta-Review

MAJOR REVISIONS REQUIRED

The paper addresses a relevant problem in human–AI interaction by proposing a framework and measurement protocol for assessing knowledge availability for AI agents in developer ecosystems. Its eleven indicators and worked examples offer a promising exploratory diagnostic approach.

My initial assessment was Reject because of concerns about the validity and reliability of the measurement protocol, the interpretation of comparative results, and the strength of the HCI contribution. However, the reviewer discussion identified a bounded and verifiable path toward acceptance. In particular, R2 specified methodological corrections and a limited reliability assessment that would be sufficient for an exploratory contribution. R1 also considered this direction acceptable, while emphasizing the need to acknowledge the limitations of interpretive coding and the absence of comprehensive inter-coder reliability validation. R3 clarified that a practitioner evaluation is not essential, provided the claims are appropriately scoped.

Therefore, I recommend MAJOR REVISIONS REQUIRED, with details on the requirements to be met, utilizing existing research materials.

Brief Review Synthesis / Analysis

Novelty. The paper offers a potentially novel perspective by examining the knowledge conditions supporting AI agents rather than focusing exclusively on retrieval success or task performance. However, the contribution needs clearer differentiation from existing approaches to information retrieval, documentation usability, and agent evaluation.

Correctness. This is the principal concern. The composite score M11 incorporates model self-rated prior knowledge (M7) in a way that can obscure the influence of retrieved evidence. Its weighting and normalization require correction and sensitivity analysis. Several indicators also depend on interpretive scoring without sufficient evidence of reliability. The reviewer discussion established that a bounded independent coding check, transparent reporting, and appropriate qualification of these limitations could support an exploratory methodological contribution.

Originality. The distinction between knowledge availability, retrieval success, and answer correctness is promising. The task-level diagnostic framework and traceable records may provide useful methodological knowledge, but the paper must establish more explicitly what the integrated framework contributes beyond its individual, established components.

Importance. Assessing the accessibility of technical knowledge to AI agents is relevant to HCI and developer-tool research. The CANN-CUDA comparison illustrates potential diagnostic applications. However, conclusions must remain specific to the tested agent and retrieval configuration, and benefits for developers or documentation teams should be presented as potential rather than empirically demonstrated.

Clarity. The manuscript is generally well structured and includes useful worked examples and supplementary materials. Nevertheless, the composite scoring procedure, evaluator responsibilities, and relationship between the indicators and reported conclusions require clearer explanation.

Required Changes

1. Revise and validate the composite measure (M11). Remove model self-rated prior knowledge (M7) from the primary composite and report it separately. Correct the normalization so that M1 = 1 (“still not found”) contributes zero to official-source support. Recalculate the main results and provide sensitivity analyses for the weighting scheme and score-band thresholds. Report the individual indicators and explain whether the main comparative findings remain robust under these changes.

2. Provide a bounded reliability assessment of the scoring procedure. Clearly identify who assigned each indicator score and which indicators required interpretive judgment. Have an independent evaluator score a subset of approximately 10-15 existing task-side records for M3, M6, M9, and M10. Report appropriate agreement statistics, discrepancies, and their resolution. If an LLM assigned the original scores, include independent human verification of the sampled records. Explicitly and prominently acknowledge that the full set of interpretive judgments has not undergone independent inter-coder reliability validation, discuss the implications of this limitation, and moderate the corresponding claims accordingly.

3. Limit and clarify the empirical conclusions. Explicitly frame the comparison between CANN and CUDA as conditional on the specific model, retrieval tools, tasks, and execution conditions tested. Distinguish observations about documentation sources from effects that may depend on the agent or retrieval configuration. Avoid generalized ecosystem rankings or claims of validated measurement performance beyond the available evidence.

4. Clarify the conceptual and HCI contribution. Position knowledge availability more precisely relative to existing concepts in information retrieval, documentation usability, and agent evaluation. Explain which diagnostic distinctions the proposed framework enables. Present the protocol as an exploratory diagnostic method rather than a fully validated measurement instrument, and describe practitioner benefits and the design illustrations as potential applications rather than demonstrated outcomes. Discuss how future developer or documentation-team evaluations could assess the framework's usefulness.

Reviewer 1 · Review (Round 1)

Revise and Resubmit

Originality

Original conceptual and methodological contribution: formalizes knowledge availability for AI agents as a distinct construct, with an 11-indicator task-level measurement protocol and cross-ecosystem diagnostic approach. Individual building blocks exist, but the integrated diagnostic workflow is novel. Lacks inter-coder reliability validation.

Correctness

Claims are appropriate for a method-oriented demonstrative study. Analysis units, scoring rules, and case interpretations are clearly documented. Limitations: no inter-coder reliability test, single agent configuration tested, M7 relies on indirect self-assessment, proposed UI designs are conceptual.

Novelty

Novel framing for HCI agent research: measures upstream knowledge-supply conditions independent of agent reasoning quality, instead of only end-task performance. The traceable failure-mode decomposition is a new methodological approach, though the underlying retrieval techniques are established.

Importance

Valuable to CHI if validated: complements agent benchmarks (e.g., SWE-bench) for research; gives practitioners an actionable documentation audit protocol; derives concrete design implications for docs and agent interfaces. Impact is conditional on further reliability and field validation.

Clarity

Well-structured and self-contained. Clearly distinguishes related constructs and separates empirical findings from design proposals. Minor drawbacks: dense composite scoring logic; some scoring details are split across text and appendix.

Figure Descriptions

The figure descriptions are adequate and follow the accessibility guidelines.

Ratings: Originality—somewhat agree; Correctness—somewhat agree; Novelty—somewhat agree; Importance—somewhat agree; Clarity of Exposition—somewhat agree. Recommendation: I recommend Revise and Resubmit.

Reviewer 2 · Review (Round 1)

Revise and Resubmit

Summary

The paper proposes “knowledge availability for AI”: the extent to which task-relevant technical knowledge can be discovered, obtained, and assessed for applicability by a given agent and tool configuration. It is operationalized as eleven indicators (M1–M11) with an evidence-record protocol and applied to 25 paired CANN/CUDA development tasks, plus one ROCm/HIP case, using a single Claude model with web search and fetch tools. CANN scores lower on the composite (0.731 vs. 0.922), particularly on version clarity, content detail, and in-depth development tasks. The paper closes with six design illustrations.

Strengths

The conceptual work is the paper's strongest part. Table 1 cleanly separates retrieval success, documentation usability, knowledge availability, answer correctness, and execution success, and the insistence on keeping observation apart from interpretation (lines 255–259) is exactly right. The worked cases D, E, A, and X show the diagnostic distinctions doing real work, in a form a documentation team could act on. The limitations section is candid, and the supplement supports transparency.

My main concern is that the paper presents a measurement method with almost no evidence that it measures reliably or validly, and several design choices undercut the paper's own commitments.

Concerns

1. The composite score. Section 3.2 states that internal model knowledge “cannot make an inaccessible external source accessible” (lines 245–247), yet M11 treats the model's self-rated prior knowledge (M7) as an independent channel with the same weight as fetched official content. Whenever M7 = 5, K = 1 regardless of the retrieved evidence. Fig. 7 shows M7 = 5 for 14 of the 25 CUDA records, so for those tasks M1–M3, M5, and M6 have no effect on M11. Self-ratings of this kind are poorly calibrated (Kadavath et al., 2022; Xiong et al., 2024). Recomputing M11 without the M7 term, using the paper's formula, gives 0.599 vs. 0.846: the gap remains, but the headline values are compressed toward the ceiling. In addition, normalizing ordinal grades as s/5 and multiplying them assumes ratio-scale properties the anchors lack (Stevens, 1946; Liddell & Kruschke, 2018); for example, M1 = 1 (“still not found”) still contributes 0.2 to official support. The weights and band cut-points are neither justified nor tested. Please report M11 with and without M7 (or treat M7 as a separate annotation, as with M9 and M10), validate M7 against a no-retrieval condition, add a sensitivity analysis on the weights, and report medians and distributions alongside the means in Table 4.

2. Reliability and coding. M3, M6, M9, and M10 are interpretive judgments, but Section 5.2 does not say whether a human, the LLM, or both assigned them. If the model graded its own retrievals and responses, documented self-preference biases apply (Panickssery et al., 2024; Zheng et al., 2023). If humans coded, please report the number of coders, whether coding was blind to ecosystem, and agreement. For a paper whose main contribution is a measurement protocol, agreement on at least a subset of records is close to essential (McDonald et al., 2019; Jacobs & Wallach, 2021).

3. Ecosystem effects vs. tool effects. Each task side was run once, with one model and a fetch tool that “summarized HTML from selected URLs” (lines 621–622). The pivotal finding for task D (line 876) is the summarizer's output, not the raw page. The paper rightly calls the construct relational, but Sections 6–7 and the conclusion (line 1347) draw ecosystem-level conclusions that this design cannot separate from tool behaviour. The questions were also posed in Chinese (Table 3), which interacts with search locale, ranking, and which third-party platforms appear; this is not discussed. Task pairs differ in scope (task A, lines 599–603), and it is unclear who wrote and validated the task requirements against which adequacy was judged. I recommend repeating each task side at least three times, adding a second retrieval configuration (e.g., a rendered-DOM fetch) for a subset of tasks, and summarizing the task requirements in the main paper. Otherwise, findings should be framed as conditional on this configuration.

4. Aggregate comparisons. The claim that debugging shows the largest gap (line 818) rests on two tasks per side. The scenario grouping in Table 6 appears post hoc (e.g., error-code diagnosis under “training and routine use”), and the “developer-role groupings” at line 571 are never defined. The descending CANN pattern is small relative to the within-group SDs. Please remove the ranking language, state the grouping rationale up front, and add bootstrap intervals if the aggregates are kept.

5. Indicators that reflect agent behaviour. M5 and M8 depend partly on the agent's own stopping decisions and move in opposite directions as searching increases, yet no retrieval budget is reported, although Section 4.2 says one should be set. Please report it and discuss this coupling.

6. HCI contribution. The method targets practitioners (lines 80–82), but none were involved, and the design illustrations are unevaluated. A methodological contribution needs evidence that it works for its intended users (Wobbrock & Kientz, 2016). Even a small session with documentation owners, or a check of whether CANN maintainers recognize the D, E, and A diagnoses, would strengthen the importance claim.

7. Related work. The paper should engage with studies of version and API-evolution failures in LLM code generation (Wang et al., 2025), information foraging in programming (Lawrance et al., 2013), and agent-readable documentation efforts such as llms.txt (Howard, 2024), which the export proposal in Section 7.2 closely resembles.

Minor Issues

  • The Fig. 9 subtitle refers to Figures 2–4; this appears to mean Figures 6–8.
  • The “—” for D's M3 is described as “unobserved” in Figs. 6–8 but “blocked” in the text and Table 4.
  • The prose in Sections 6–7 is heavily nominalized (e.g., lines 952–953). Concrete statements about specific pages, steps, and versions would help readers.

Length

The paper is standard length, which suits its scope. However, Section 4.1 overlaps with Figs. 2–4, Table 5 with Table 6, and Fig. 6 with Fig. 9, and the six unevaluated design figures could be reduced to two or three. Cutting 15–20% would make room for the validation evidence requested above.

Recommendation

The framing is useful and the worked cases are strong, but the measurement apparatus is not yet validated well enough to support the comparative claims. Separating model prior knowledge from evidence in the composite, reporting reliability and run-to-run variance, testing the sensitivity of the weights, and limiting conclusions to the tested configuration would make this a solid methodological contribution.

Reviewer 3 · Review (Round 1)

Either Reject or Revise and Resubmit

This paper introduces the concept of knowledge availability for AI as the extent to which technical knowledge can be discovered, obtained, and assessed for applicability under agentic conditions. The authors operationalize this construct through 11 indicators and apply the resulting protocol to a comparative case study of CANN and CUDA development ecosystems. In development tasks, the paper examines differences in source discoverability, content accessibility, documentation detail, version clarity, acquisition effort, and the actionability of agent responses. The authors use these findings to derive implications for technical documentation and AI-agent interaction design.

Overall, the paper addresses a relevant problem in HCI, as AI agents increasingly mediate human developers’ access to technical knowledge. Specifically, the paper’s attempt to move beyond answer quality and examine the knowledge conditions underlying AI-generated guidance is appreciated. However, there are some main areas of concerns for the construct proposed by the paper: it would benefit from a stronger justification, clearer evidence of the reliability and validity of its measurement protocol, and more careful treatment of the comparative findings and their implications.

1. The construct of knowledge availability is defined as “the extent to which task-relevant technical knowledge can be discovered, obtained, and assessed for applicability by a specified agent and tool configuration under stated retrieval conditions” (Section 3.1).

The description suggests that the construct’s novelty lies in connecting familiar observations, such as search rank, page accessibility, documentation detail, and version labels, to task-specific requirements. It is unclear whether knowledge availability is a distinct conceptual construct or a useful umbrella term for a set of existing documentation and retrieval properties, and precisely how it differs from existing concepts. A comparison table could clarify the unit of analysis, dimensions measured, and the output of each approach. The authors should explain what new analytical distinction their framework enables that would be difficult to obtain from these existing approaches individually.

2. In Figure 2 the authors define ordinal scoring anchors for official-source discoverability (M1), content accessibility (M2), content detail (M3), and version clarity (M4). Some of these raise questions about whether the scores measure the intended construct or instead reflect characteristics of the retrieval tools and the knowledge source. For example, the scoring anchors appear to reward static rendering over server-side rendering even when the content is successfully retrieved in both cases. Similarly, M1 uses search-result position and query refinement as indicators of discoverability. The authors should explain why static and server-rendered pages warrant different scores when both return the required content. A sensitivity analysis using alternative search-result positions or equivalent retrieval outcomes would help establish whether these scoring decisions materially affect the findings. The paper would also benefit from a clearer mapping between each score and its intended interpretation. In particular, the authors should state which indicators represent properties of the knowledge ecosystem, which represent properties of the agent and tools, and which represent interactions between the two.

3. Table 2 introduces a composite confidence score combining support from official material, third-party material, and model prior knowledge, with adjustments for version clarity and acquisition effort. While the authors cautions against treating the score as a calibrated probability of correctness, the composite remains central to the results in Section 6.1 and Table 5.

The underlying indicators include search-result rank, acquisition outcomes, documentation detail, … and more. The paper needs to establish why the chosen aggregation reflects a meaningful overall measure of knowledge support. Consider providing a more explicit rationale for the composite formula. Also consider a sensitivity analysis that recalculates the principal results under plausible alternative weighting schemes and, where appropriate, without the composite score, and see if the CANN-CUDA ordering and the main workflow-level patterns remain stable.

4. In Section 5.2, the paper describes the use of four task batches, with later batches handled through parallel subagents and observations consolidated in the main session. It also states that the analysis combined recorded queries, tool-returned content and summaries, structured observations, and scoring rationales.

The description does not establish whether different evaluators would independently assign the same scores to the same evidence. This matters because several indicators require judgement, and the composite score inherits uncertainty from those judgements.

Consider clarify which indicators were scored directly from logged events and which required interpretive coding. They should report whether any observations were independently scored and, if so, provide an appropriate agreement measure or a transparent account of disagreements and adjudication. Also, publishing a few fully worked examples that connect raw observations, task requirements, scoring decisions, and final diagnostic interpretations would further improve reproducibility.

5. Currently the paper does not directly demonstrate that using the framework improves developers' decisions or helps teams prioritise interventions more effectively. This is not necessarily a flaw but the distinction matters for its HCI contribution. The study demonstrates that the protocol can organize observations and generate diagnoses, but it does not yet establish that those diagnoses lead to better human outcomes. I recommend the authors put heavier weight on the potential benefits this framework has on human developers, clearly lay out the contributions in this domain.

Minor comments

1. Section 4.3: Please explain the distinction between the scores for “Static body retrievable” and “SSR body retrievable.” If both yield complete task-relevant content, clarify why the rendering mechanism should affect the score.

2. Section 4.3: For the acquisition-effort formula, please clarify whether the coefficients are empirically calibrated or chosen as heuristic relative costs, and examine whether alternative weights change the main results.

3. Section 4.3: It would be good to have a worked example showing how the model self-rating and technological-change deduction yield the final score, including how the technology's rate of change is classified.

4. Section 6: It would be good to report task-level variability alongside the workflow means and clarify the extent to which the composite differences are driven by particular indicators or tasks.

Ratings: Originality—somewhat agree; Correctness—somewhat agree; Novelty—neither agree nor disagree; Importance—somewhat disagree; Clarity of Exposition—somewhat agree. Recommendation: I can go with either Reject or Revise and Resubmit.

Reviewer 1 · 总体支持，强调边界透明

建议重投

R1 认为论文提出了一个有潜力的新视角：把 AI Agent 所处的知识环境作为研究对象，而不只看检索成功或任务表现。11 项任务级协议和跨生态诊断有整合上的新意，工作案例也能展示诊断框架的作用。

主要肯定

  • 把外部知识可得性与 Agent 推理能力、最终答案正确性分开，具有 HCI 研究价值。
  • 案例和记录有可追溯性；论文结构较完整，局限性也较坦诚。
  • 方法可补充现有 Agent benchmark，并可能帮助文档团队审查知识入口。

保留意见

  • 没有评审者间信度检验。
  • 只测试一种 Agent 配置。
  • M7 依赖模型间接自评先验知识。
  • 设计示例尚未实际评估。

解读：R1 的态度较正面，接受探索性、方法型论文的定位，但希望论文不要把“提出并演示了方法”写成“已充分验证的方法”。

Reviewer 2 · 最具体的方法学审查

与 AC 要求高度相关

R2 认可概念框架、案例和材料透明度，但认为论文把测量方法作为主要贡献，却几乎没有证据证明它可以稳定测量。以下意见中，AC 已吸收的部分属于本轮硬要求；其余是审稿人提出的增强建议。

1. M11 与 M7 先验自评

  • 论文说模型内部知识不能让不可得的外部来源变得可得，但 M11 又把 M7 当作与检索内容等权的独立渠道。
  • 按现公式，M7=5 时可让综合值 K=1，无论外部检索证据如何。R2 指出，25 个 CUDA 记录中有 14 个 M7=5，因此外部指标在这些记录中的作用会被抵消。
  • R2 重算了不含 M7 的示例结果：CANN/CUDA 从 0.731/0.922 变为约 0.599/0.846；差距仍在，但总值更低。正式修改应使用原始数据和冻结后的最终公式重新计算，不应直接把审稿人的示例数当成最终结果。

2. 序数评分、权重和阈值

  • 将等级用 s/5 线性缩放并相乘，隐含等级可按比例解释的假设；分档锚点并未证明具备这种比例尺度性质。
  • M1=1“仍未找到”仍贡献 0.2 官方支持，与语义不符。
  • 权重和分档切点尚无论证或敏感性验证；应比较合理替代方案，并同时报告中位数、分布与均值。

3. 可靠性与编码过程

  • M3、M6、M9、M10 属于解释性判断；方法部分未说明评分者是人、LLM，还是两者。
  • 如果是模型给自己检索结果打分，可能有自偏好问题；如果由人打分，则需交代评分者数量、是否对生态盲法以及一致性。
  • R2 认为对方法型论文而言，至少对一部分记录做一致性检查非常重要。AC 将其收敛为约 10–15 份既有记录的独立复评。

4. 生态差异与工具差异可能混淆

  • 每个任务侧只运行一次，使用一个模型与一个会总结所选 URL HTML 的抓取工具；重要发现可能反映摘要器行为，而非原始页面内容。
  • 中文提问会影响搜索地区、排名和第三方来源；任务对之间的范围差异、任务要求由谁制定和校验也需交代。
  • R2 的建议：每侧至少重复三次，并对部分任务加入第二种检索配置（例如渲染 DOM）。AC 的要求：将结论明确限定在本研究的配置条件内；AC 没有把全量重复运行列为必需修改。

5. 聚合比较与分组

  • “调试差异最大”等表述只依赖每侧两个任务；部分分组看起来像事后形成，开发者角色分组也未定义。
  • 建议去掉排名式描述，预先说明分组逻辑；若保留聚合，提供 bootstrap 区间并同时呈现任务层面的变异。

6. 指标受 Agent 自身行为影响

  • M5 与 M8 部分依赖 Agent 何时停止搜索，搜索增加时两项指标可能朝相反方向变化。
  • 论文提到应设置检索预算，却没有报告实际预算；需要补充预算并讨论该耦合关系。

7. HCI 贡献、相关工作与篇幅

  • 没有从业者参与，也没有评估设计示例，因此不能证明框架已经改善开发者决策或团队优先级判断。
  • 建议补充 API 弃用与 LLM 代码生成、程序员信息觅食、Agent 可读文档（如 llms.txt）相关工作。
  • 建议删减重复图表和六个未评估设计示例中的一部分，为验证证据腾出篇幅；指出约 15–20% 的压缩空间。

解读：R2 提出的“重复运行三次、加第二种检索配置”等能加强外部效度，但耗时较大。应优先完成 AC 明确要求的 M11 修正、敏感性分析和有限复评；对未采纳的扩展建议，要在回复信中解释其与当前探索性研究范围的关系，并收窄结论。

Reviewer 3 · 追问构念边界与指标效度

可接受任一建议

R3 认为 AI 知识可用性与 HCI 相关，也认可研究从“回答质量”转向“回答背后的知识条件”。主要疑问是：新构念的分析价值是否讲清楚，指标究竟测量知识源、检索工具还是二者交互。

1. 知识可用性的独特贡献是什么？

发现性、页面访问、文档细节和版本标签等都是已有研究会关注的性质。R3 希望论文说明把它们整合后能揭示什么新的分析区分，并建议用对照表与信息检索、文档可用性、Agent 评测比较分析单位、测量维度和输出。

2. 指标可能奖励了工具实现方式，而非任务结果

  • 如果静态页面和服务端渲染页面都成功返回完整任务相关内容，为什么评分应不同？
  • M1 用搜索排名和改写查询衡量发现性，但这可能受搜索工具、语言区域影响。
  • 应逐指标说明其主要测量对象是知识生态属性、Agent/工具属性，还是交互结果，并考虑替代评分规则下结论是否变化。

3. 综合公式的依据与稳健性

虽然论文说明综合分不是校准后的正确概率，它仍是结果章节的中心量。R3 要求解释聚合为何代表有意义的知识支持，并考虑不同权重或不用综合分时，CANN/CUDA 顺序与工作流模式是否稳定。

4. 评分可复现性

  • 区分从日志直接读取的评分与解释性编码。
  • 报告独立评分、一致性或分歧裁决。
  • 发布若干完整工作例子，连接原始观察、任务要求、评分理由与最终诊断。

5. HCI 效果主张应保持准确

本研究展示框架能组织观察、生成诊断；尚未直接证明这些诊断能改善开发者决策或团队干预效果。R3 明确说这不一定是缺陷，但论文应把两类贡献区分开。

补充细节

  • 解释 acquisition-effort 公式系数是经验校准值还是启发式相对成本。
  • 给出模型自评与技术变化扣分的完整算例，包括技术变化率分档。
  • 报告任务层面的变异，并解释综合差异由哪些任务或指标驱动。
